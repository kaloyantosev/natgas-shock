Does a bigger gas run-up mean a bigger fall?

In real time: what followed each gas move

Peaks are only known in hindsight. The usable test runs forward: on every trading day, take the past-month gas move and check whether the S&P 500 fell 10% or more within the next 3 months. The p-value compares each bucket with randomly shifted days.

Methodology

Definitions
  1. Correction = S&P 500 close at least 10% below a closing high (20% = bear market). An episode ends at a new high or after a 25% rally off its low, so the 2010 and 2011 corrections, which happened below the 2007 high, count separately.
  2. Gas run-up = Henry Hub front-month (NG=F) move over the 21 trading days to the S&P peak. Roll-adjusted: the day the series switches contract is dropped, because the gap between two contracts is not a price move.
  3. Forward buckets are fixed at −10/0/+10/+20%, set before looking at results, so no future information goes into them.
  4. p-values come from circular shifts of the event dates (keeping clustering and overlapping windows), correlations from permutation tests.
Limitations
  • Ten corrections is a small sample. Several statistics are tested on this page, so a single p-value near 0.05 is weak evidence.
  • Stocks themselves rally into a peak, and commodities tend to rise in the same risk-on mood. Gas rising beforehand may be a symptom of that, not a cause.
  • Daily outcomes overlap: one bear market makes many neighbouring days count as "followed by a 10% fall".
  • The sample starts in 2001, so the 2000–2002 bear market is not included. Henry Hub is a US price; European gas (TTF) is not covered.
