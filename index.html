Methods

Definitions
  1. Major decline = S&P 500 close at least 15% below a closing high (20%+ = bear market). An episode ends at a new high or after a 25% rally off its low, so the 2010 and 2011 declines, which happened below the 2007 high, count separately.
  2. Gas run-up = Henry Hub front-month (NG=F) move over the 21 trading days to the S&P peak. Roll-adjusted: the day the series switches contract is dropped, because the gap between two contracts is not a price move.
  3. Real-time test uses fixed buckets (−10/0/+10/+20%) set before looking at results, and a 6-month horizon, so nothing from the future goes into it.
  4. p-values come from circular shifts of the event dates, which keep clustering and overlapping windows intact; correlations from permutation tests.
Limitations
  • Seven declines is a very small sample. Several statistics are tested here, so any single p-value near 0.05 is weak evidence.
  • Peaks are dated with hindsight. Only the real-time test (section 6) reflects what an investor could have known.
  • Stocks rally into a peak, and commodities tend to rise in the same risk-on mood. Gas rising beforehand may be a symptom of the late-cycle rally, not a cause of the fall.
  • The sample starts in 2001, so the 2000–2002 bear market is not covered. Henry Hub is a US price; European gas (TTF) is not covered.
